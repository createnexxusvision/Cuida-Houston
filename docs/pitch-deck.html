Cuida HOU.Una red de cuidado infantil para Houston

A bilingual childcare network with two sides. Parents find licensed care they can reach. Women in childcare deserts get a guided path to open licensed home daycares.

A desert is a ZIP code with more than three young children of working parents for every licensed seat. Harris County ZIPs 77093 and 77506 have 0 to 5 seats per 100 children.

Children at Risk reports that parents, and mothers especially, are leaving the workforce when they can't find care.

Why it hasn't been solved04

The data exists. The seats don't, and the path to more seats is hidden.Los datos existen. Los cupos, no.

Search tools stop at search

The state's Texas Child Care Availability Portal already shows open seats by age. It can't add seats where there are none, and it doesn't plan around a parent's bus route or shift.

Supply is the real gap

In desert ZIP codes there are more than 3 young children of working parents for every licensed seat. Better search can't fix that. Houston needs more licensed providers inside those ZIP codes.

The licensing path is hard to see

Opening a home daycare means state minimum standards, inspections and forms. The women best placed to do it often don't know it's an option, or where to start in their language.

The subsidy waitlist is set by funding, not seats. New providers add seats where families live; they don't shorten the waitlist.

Still to check before the pitch: how fully the state portal works in Spanish, and what share of current home providers are Black or Latina women.

The solution05

One network, two doors.Una red, dos puertas.

Door 1 · Para familias

Find care you can actually get to

  • Search licensed centers and homes by ZIP, child's age and shift hours
  • Filter for subsidy acceptance and Spanish-speaking staff
  • See which options sit on your METRO route to work
  • View each provider's state inspection history
Door 2 · Para proveedoras

Open a licensed home daycare

  • Pick the right permit, starting with a registered home (up to 12 children, 6 unrelated during school hours)
  • Follow a bilingual, step-by-step checklist to state approval
  • See where your ZIP ranks for unmet demand
  • Get connected to local support partners and the families waiting nearby

Each new provider in a desert ZIP adds seats for working parents and creates a woman-owned small business.

The solution · how it works06

How it works

Step 1 · Map the gap

Combine open data by ZIP

Merge state licensing records with Census counts of young children, working mothers and language spoken at home. Rank every Houston ZIP by unmet need.

Step 2 · Match families

Search in English or Spanish

Parents search by age, hours, subsidy status and transit. The tool shows the nearest real options, with links to inspection records.

Step 3 · Grow supply

Guide new providers

In the ZIPs with the biggest gap, the provider path walks women through licensing and hands them off to partners who train and support providers.

Prototype stack: Next.js on AWS Amplify + Supabase (Postgres), with nightly AWS Lambda pulls from the state licensing API.

Sustain and grow08

How we keep it going

Cost to run

The prototype runs on free tiers of existing tools. A pilot needs hosting, Spanish-language content review and one part-time community coordinator.

[Team to set pilot budget]

What keeps data fresh

Licensing data refreshes automatically from the state API. New providers and partners report seat openings, so the network doesn't depend on one person's spreadsheet.

How we track progress

  • Early: searches, referrals, provider sign-ups, licenses in progress
  • Later: new licensed seats in desert ZIPs, mothers placed in care and back at work

Results go to partners and the City through a live public dashboard, not an annual report.
